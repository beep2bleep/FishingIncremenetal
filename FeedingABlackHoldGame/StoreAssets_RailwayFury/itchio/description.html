Railway Fury — Demo

One more dive. One more upgrade. One more shot at the boss.

Railway Fury is an incremental corridor shooter. Push down a dangerous railway, shoot monsters, dodge fire waves and collect the loot they leave behind. Every run pays for the next one.

Spend your gold and gems on damage, health, armor, returning daggers and abilities. Experiment with the upgrade tree, claim achievement rewards, then head back down the tracks stronger than before.

Two ways to play

  • Free Aim: aim with the mouse, strafe around attacks and fire waves, and move to collect your payouts.
  • Classic: shoot straight ahead and steer through the corridor. Each mode has its own upgrade tree and saved progress.

The hallway itself drains your health, so standing between enemies and dodging their attacks is only part of surviving. Press Shift to toggle double speed when you feel ready to push harder.

What is in this demo?

  • The first railway, including its minibosses and final boss.
  • Permanent upgrades, achievement rewards and both play modes.
  • Three selectable art and audio styles, including the illustrated presentation shown here.
  • Saved progress and an active gameplay timer.

The demo ends when you defeat the first railway's final boss. You can replay Level 1 afterward. Crown upgrades, the three later levels and the two ending choices are reserved for the full game.

Controls

  • A / D or Left / Right: strafe.
  • Mouse + left click: aim and shoot in Free Aim.
  • Shift: toggle double speed.
  • Esc: pause, settings and return to upgrades.
  • 3–6: activate abilities after unlocking them.

Play in your browser or download for Windows

A keyboard and mouse are recommended. The browser version requires WebGL 2.0; a current Chrome, Edge or Firefox is recommended. If browser performance is low, try the Windows download.

PC supports English, Spanish, German, Portuguese, French, Italian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Polish, Turkish, Thai, Indonesian, Czech, Catalan and Vietnamese. The web version omits Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai because of font compatibility limitations.
